# Genre Vocabulary — Per-Question Variants

Reference for adapting friction questions by genre. Each trigger question has 4 natural-language versions.

---

## 1. Genre Tag Definitions

- **`narrative-fiction`** — Novels, short stories, novellas; invented characters and events.
- **`memoir`** — First-person retrospective nonfiction organized around a wound or reckoning.
- **`creative-nonfiction`** — Reported or researched nonfiction with literary structure (long-form journalism, immersion, essay-as-investigation).
- **`long-form-essay`** — Argument-driven or inquiry-driven nonfiction organized around a question, not a narrative arc.

---

## 2. Friction Question Variants

### core_tension

| Genre | Question |
|---|---|
| Fiction | "What force is working against what your protagonist wants most?" |
| Memoir | "What resists the understanding you're trying to reach about this experience?" |
| CNF | "What tension exists between what you expected to find and what the material actually reveals?" |
| Essay | "What challenges the central claim you're trying to make?" |

### stakes

| Genre | Question |
|---|---|
| Fiction | "If your protagonist fails completely, what is permanently lost or broken?" |
| Memoir | "If you never reach the understanding you're after, what stays unresolved in your life?" |
| CNF | "If this story goes untold, what does the reader never learn that matters?" |
| Essay | "If your argument fails to convince, what real-world consequence follows?" |

### inciting_event

| Genre | Question |
|---|---|
| Fiction | "What specific event forces your protagonist into this story rather than letting them stay in their ordinary life?" |
| Memoir | "What moment made it impossible to keep living inside the old story you told yourself?" |
| CNF | "What made you start looking into this — what wouldn't leave you alone?" |
| Essay | "What provoked this essay — what observation or contradiction demanded you write it?" |

### protagonist_desire (want)

| Genre | Question |
|---|---|
| Fiction | "What does your protagonist want badly enough to act on?" |
| Memoir | "What are you trying to understand or make peace with by writing this?" |
| CNF | "What question is driving your investigation — what do you need to find out?" |
| Essay | "What claim are you trying to make, and why does it matter to you personally?" |

### protagonist_wound (misbelief)

| Genre | Question |
|---|---|
| Fiction | "What does your protagonist believe about themselves or the world that isn't true?" |
| Memoir | "What story did you tell yourself for years that this memoir is finally questioning?" |
| CNF | "What assumption did you bring to this subject that the reporting complicated?" |
| Essay | "What comfortable position does your argument require the reader to give up?" |

### character_arc_type

| Genre | Question |
|---|---|
| Fiction | "How does your protagonist change — what do they believe at the end that they didn't at the start?" |
| Memoir | "What distance have you earned from the person you were in these events?" |
| CNF | "How did the investigation change what you thought you knew?" |
| Essay | "Where does your thinking arrive that it couldn't have reached at the beginning?" |

---

## 3. Genre-Normative Structural Differences

Different genres resolve the "misbelief-equivalent" differently:

- **Fiction:** Misbelief is **overcome**. The protagonist sheds their false belief through the story's events.
- **Memoir:** The Lie the Self Told is **exposed**. The narrator gains distance from the survival story they held.
- **Creative Nonfiction:** The Reporter's Complicity is **interrogated**. The writer's assumptions are confronted by the material.
- **Long-Form Essay:** The Governing Contradiction may be **held permanently**. This is genre-normative, NOT a design gap. Do not force resolution where the genre does not require it.

---

## 4. Hybrid Genre Handling

- Writer selects one primary genre tag at session intake.
- Primary tag governs ALL gate requirements, character frame vocabulary, and friction question language.
- Secondary genre characteristics can be noted in `story-manifest.md` as a freeform field.
- At any gate, the writer can request KB resources from a secondary genre tag — writer-initiated override only.
