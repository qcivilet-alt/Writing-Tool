## Structure

act_structure: Three-act, compressed timeline (~3 weeks)
opening_condition: "Maren living in self-imposed exile, working boat repairs, avoiding the water. Callum's death anniversary approaching."
ending_type: "Ambiguous — she sees the full pattern, understands what Callum died for, but the truth is bigger than one person can expose. She surfaces with evidence but no certainty it will matter."

inciting_incident_placed: "Act 1, Chapter 2 — Maren finds Callum's hidden dive log in a box of his things she's been avoiding. The log contains coordinates and sketches that don't match any structure she was ever assigned to weld."

midpoint_reversal: "Act 2, Chapter 6 — Maren dives to the coordinates and sees the pattern from below for the first time. It's not damage or decay. It's deliberate. The structures she maintained for 30 years were always part of something else. Reframes the entire story from 'who killed Callum' to 'what did we build?'"

climax_sketch: "Act 3, Chapter 9 — Maren confronts Pryce with the evidence at the decommissioned rig. He doesn't deny it — he's terrified of whoever commissioned the pattern. The climax isn't a fight; it's Pryce showing her the full blueprint and asking her whether she really wants to be the person who brings this up from the deep."

## Key Turning Points
1. Dive log discovery (inciting incident)
2. First dive since Callum's death — physically re-entering the water (Act 1 climax)
3. Seeing the pattern from below (midpoint)
4. Meeting another diver who saw it and was silenced (Act 2 escalation)
5. Pryce reveals the full blueprint (climax)
6. Maren surfaces with evidence, alone, uncertain (resolution)

## Pacing Notes
Short chapters. Alternating above-water tension (investigation, isolation, paranoia) with below-water sequences (sensory, meditative, terrifying). The underwater scenes slow down; the surface scenes accelerate.

## Open Structure Questions
- How many chapters? Estimating 10-12.
- Does Maren have an ally or does she work alone?
- What exactly IS the pattern? (The writer may not need to fully explain it — the question may be more powerful than the answer.)
